Stat5810: Jingjing Wang



Homework 6 - Evaluation for Other 4 lectures

Lecture 1: Basic Graphics

In this lecture the web-based textbook was used mainly to show the concepts, graphics, examples of histogram and boxplot. The lecture is very clear, but I think maybe a little bit fast for the beginners. And I think if there were more blackboard work, it would be better.

The interesting thing in the lecture is that the interactive graphics used to show examples of how histograms and boxplots changed with different data sets, which is more effective than pure "chalk & textbook".

The disadvantage of the lecture is that students did not have time to do some hands-on experience and excises. And another thing is that it did not show clearly how to get into some web pages, so that sometimes the students got lost.

Lecture 2: Confidence Intervals & Hypothesis testing

In this lecture web-based textbook was used totally to show the concepts and the theories of confidence intervals & hypothesis testing. The description was very clear, but a little bit fast for a beginner to follow. And since the web page is full of words and graphics, the students are easy to be lost at the beginning. So I think it would be better if the instructor could highlight the description and conclusions that were being talked about.

The interesting thing is that the instructor used the data from the real life (such as the coming president election) in the examples on confidence intervals on a mean and proportion to illustrate why usually large sample size was chosen to obtain data. This is good since this kind of examples usually can give students deep impression.

The bad thing is that there was no hands-on experience for the students. And in the example for hypothesis testing, the result was just rejection of something, no description of the alternative hypothesis. The students may be confused. So I think sometimes instructors should not depend totally on the web-based textbook. In my opinion, the lecture is less effective.

Lecture 3: Probability

The instructor used web-based textbook as an aid in the lecture. The lecture began with the instruction of probability on the blackboard, which was very clear for students. I think the advantage of "chalk and blackboard" is that it is easy for students to follow you, rather than being lost in the web page ocean.

Then the web-based exercises were chosen for the students to practice. The exercises are ready on the web and many of them have interactive graphics and immediate answers so that they are welcome for both instructors and students. But in my opinion, most of the excises are basic ones, not appropriate for in-depth study.

Generally, It is very effective to combine "chalk & blackboard" and web-based excises in the lecture.

Lecture 4: Linear Regression

Like the Lecture3, "chalk & blackboard" and web-based textbook were combined in the lecture. These make the lecture very clear and effective. The example using the speed of a car on the freeway from Logan to Salt Lake City to compute the distance given the time was interesting, since it used data from our own experience. Hands-on experience using interactive graphics on the web made students have chance and interests to practice by themselves to learn what is the best fit of regression to a data set.

The disadvantage is that it took too much time fitting "by hand", which is not the essential of the linear regression. So there was less time left to talk about the ordinary least square. So, I think the time allocation is not very good in the lecture.

  • None of the lectures above talked about something that motivates students to learn statistics.
  • In my opinion, the presentation of basic graphics was more effective than the pure "chalk and textbook" approach that is still used for many introductory statistics classes.
